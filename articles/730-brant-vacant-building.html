730 Brant sat empty for more than a decade. Then it caught fire.

A redevelopment path existed in 2016. Ten years later, the old building was still there. The public record explains what was allowed, but not why nothing replaced it.

The strange part about 730 Brant Street is not that a vacant building eventually had a fire.

It is that a redevelopment route had already been opened a decade earlier.

On Jan. 25, 2016, Burlington's Committee of Adjustment approved most of the zoning relief needed for a proposed four-storey condominium with ground-floor retail at 730 and 760 Brant. In February 2026, fire crews entered the still-vacant commercial building after flames and propane-tank explosions brought 10 trucks and 33 firefighters to the property.

Those two records are easy to find. What happened in the ten years between them is much harder.

Still unansweredThe public sources reviewed do not explain why the approved project did not proceed.

The 2016 approval was real, but limited

The committee approved changes involving vehicle access, setbacks, landscaping, underground parking and the total parking supply. It refused only the request to reduce accessible parking.

That moved the proposal closer to being buildable. It did not force construction to begin, create a deadline, guarantee financing or require the owner to demolish the existing building.

This distinction matters because “approved” can sound much more final than it is. Planning permission answers what may be built. It does not explain whether the project can be financed, sold, redesigned or abandoned.

Then the cost of waiting became visible

In February 2026, a fire on the main floor sent 10 trucks and 33 Burlington firefighters to the property. It took about four hours to control. Propane tanks stored inside exploded, no injuries were reported and the damage was estimated at $100,000.

BurlingtonToday reported that the building had been vacant for more than a decade and had previously housed a rehabilitation clinic and beauty salon. A fire department representative said the property had been vandalized several times and suspected an intruder or unhoused person may have started the fire.

That was not a final cause finding. Burlington's fire-prevention unit was still investigating.

What the evidence does not support: there is no basis in the public records reviewed to claim the fire was connected to insurance, debt or an effort to escape property costs. Those claims would require actual fire-investigation, ownership and insurance evidence.

Who owns it, and why did the project stop?

Those are the two obvious questions. They are also the two questions the easily available public record does not answer.

Registered ownership is established through Ontario's land-title system, not an old planning post or a business directory. Burlington News has not completed a current parcel-register search, so naming a current owner here would be guessing.

The records reviewed also do not show whether the 2016 applicant later sought a building permit, whether the project was redesigned, whether financing failed or whether the property changed hands.

The record shows what Burlington allowed in 2016. It does not show who stopped, when they stopped or why.

Is it Burlington's longest-abandoned building?

That is a good social-media hook and a bad factual claim unless somebody can prove it.

The City does not publish a ranking of vacant buildings by the date they became vacant. So Burlington News cannot honestly call 730 Brant the city's longest-abandoned property.

What can be verified is still unusual enough: a prominent Brant Street property was reported vacant for more than a decade, a redevelopment route existed in 2016, and the old structure remained in place when fire crews arrived ten years later.

What comes next: the records that would complete this story are the current parcel register, active building or demolition permits, any property-standards orders and documentation explaining what happened to the 2016 project. This article should be updated when those records are obtained.
