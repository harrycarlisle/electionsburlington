Five people are running for mayor of Burlington. Now the race gets clearer.

The City’s registered list now shows five mayoral candidates, unless a statutory change appears.

By Burlington NewsAug. 25, 20264-minute read

Election coverage gets much more useful once the field stops changing.

Burlington's municipal nomination deadline passed Aug. 21. The City's registered-candidate list now shows five people running for mayor.

That does not tell voters who should win. It does tell them who deserves comparison.

What changed: before nominations closed, potential candidates could still enter the race. Now the mayoral field is defined by the City's official registered list, subject only to statutory changes such as a withdrawal or disqualification.

The next question is not who is running. It is how they differ.

A candidate list is only the first layer. The useful work now is comparing what each person wants to do on taxes, housing, development, transit, public safety and city spending, then checking those claims against their records and source documents.

That is why Burlington News treats the City's list as the starting point rather than the story's conclusion.

Ward races are separate

The mayor is elected city-wide. Councillor races depend on your ward, and Burlington is using new ward boundaries in 2026.

So a resident can correctly identify all five mayoral candidates and still be looking at the wrong councillor contest if they assume their old ward has not changed.

What voters should do next

First, verify your ward and voting information through the City's MyVote tools. Then compare the mayoral candidates on the same issues rather than reading five disconnected campaign pages.

Burlington News' election guide is designed around that second step: what each candidate is proposing, where their positions differ, and what the underlying sources actually say.

Election dates: voting starts Oct. 14. Election Day is Oct. 26, 2026.
