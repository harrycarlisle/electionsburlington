What 26,503 fish revealed about a damaged marsh.

The Fishway handled tens of thousands of fish in 2025. The surprising part is that a big number can still describe an ecosystem struggling to recover.

By Burlington NewsAugust 25, 20265-minute read

At first glance, 26,503 sounds like a recovery story.

That is how many fish Royal Botanical Gardens staff handled at the Cootes Paradise Fishway in 2025. But the Fishway is not a turnstile where more fish automatically means a healthier marsh.

It is a checkpoint designed to do two things at once: keep large common carp out of Cootes Paradise and allow staff to move, count and study native fish travelling between Lake Ontario and spawning habitat.

What the 2025 count showed
  • 26,503 fish handled at the Fishway
  • White sucker became the most abundant recorded species for the first time
  • Bowfin and white sucker increased
  • Common carp, goldfish, rudd and brown bullhead declined
  • More than 100 salmon were observed spawning upstream in Spencer Creek

The species matter more than the total

A restored marsh should not simply contain a lot of fish. It should support the right mix of native species.

That is why RBG's comparison between Grindstone Marsh and Cootes Paradise is so revealing. Grindstone was dominated by native fish such as sunfish. Cootes Paradise remained much more heavily influenced by introduced Eurasian species, especially goldfish.

The Fishway can keep adult carp out. It cannot by itself fix the water conditions that decide which young fish survive once they are inside the marsh.

Why goldfish can win in bad water

During hot summer periods, excess nutrients and decomposition can push dissolved oxygen close to zero. Goldfish tolerate those conditions unusually well. Many native fish do not.

That creates an ecological trap in the numbers: a marsh can produce huge numbers of young fish while still favouring species associated with degraded conditions.

Then the plants came back

For part of June and July 2025, aquatic plants spread across Cootes Paradise from end to end for the first time since the 1930s.

That is a more hopeful signal. Aquatic vegetation provides habitat, stabilizes sediments and can help shift the marsh toward a healthier state.

The transformation did not last untouched. Summer heat brought large rafts of algae. RBG nevertheless expects many of the plants to survive and continue expanding.

The Fishway can only solve one part of the problem

The structure is extremely good at controlling carp and measuring migration. It cannot stop nutrient loading, improve every upstream water source or guarantee enough oxygen through the hottest part of summer.

That is what makes the 26,503 number useful. It is not a score. It is a window into what is changing and what still is not.

The simple takeaway: the Fishway is helping restore the marsh, but recovery depends on water quality across the whole watershed. A high fish count can contain both good news and a warning.
