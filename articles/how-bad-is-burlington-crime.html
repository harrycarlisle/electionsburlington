How bad is Burlington's crime, really?

The broadest comparable police data says Burlington sits inside one of Ontario's lowest-crime large policing areas. But that answer gets less comforting when you look at what changed inside the total.

By Burlington NewsAugust 25, 20266-minute read

If you ask whether Burlington is getting dangerous, there is an annoying problem with the easiest statistic: Burlington does not get its own Crime Severity Index.

Burlington is policed by Halton Regional Police along with Oakville, Milton and Halton Hills. Statistics Canada therefore publishes the cleanest police-service comparison at the Halton level, not as a Burlington-only score.

That means we cannot honestly put a number beside “Burlington” and pretend it describes only this city.

We can still use Halton to answer the broader question.

The clearest signal: Halton's overall Crime Severity Index was 31.2 in 2024. Among the large Ontario police services compared by Halton police, that was the lowest figure in the group.

The gap is large

The Crime Severity Index, or CSI, is not a raw crime count. It gives more weight to more serious offences, making it useful for comparing places with different populations and mixes of crime.

Halton's 2024 CSI was 31.2. Durham was 47.1. York was 53.9. Peel was 56.4. Hamilton was 66.2, Toronto 68.7 and Waterloo 72.7.

So the broad picture is not ambiguous: Burlington sits inside an unusually low-crime large policing region.

That is also why a frightening individual incident does not automatically prove a crime wave. Headlines show what happened. A rate tells you how common it is.

Then the 2025 categories split in different directions

Halton police recorded 15,673 total crimes in 2025, down 1.8% from 15,967 in 2024.

Property crime fell more clearly, down 4.3%. Auto theft dropped 20.1%, break-and-enter fell 10.8%, and theft under $5,000 fell 5.5%.

If the story stopped there, “crime fell” would sound reasonable.

But violent crime rose 1.8%, from 2,377 reported incidents to 2,420. Assaults increased 2.7%. Robberies were unchanged at 132. Halton recorded three homicides in 2025 after recording none in 2024.

Fraud also moved the wrong way, reaching 2,537 reported incidents, up 12.7%.

The useful answer is not simply “up” or “down.” Property crime and auto theft improved. Violent crime edged higher. Fraud rose. A total can hide all three movements at once.

Why Toronto is not a perfect comparison

Toronto and Hamilton have police services whose published statistics line up much more closely with their namesake cities. Halton covers four municipalities.

That makes it misleading to label Halton's 31.2 CSI as “Burlington's CSI” and rank it beside Toronto as though the geographies were identical.

The regional comparison still tells us something important about Burlington's overall policing environment. It cannot tell us whether one Burlington neighbourhood has more break-ins than one neighbourhood in Oakville.

For that, more local incident and neighbourhood-level data is the better tool.

So how worried should a Burlington resident be?

The strongest comparable data does not support the idea that Burlington is experiencing crime on the same scale as Toronto or Hamilton.

It also does not support complacency. A low regional score does not mean every category is improving, and it does not make an individual victim's experience less real.

The more useful way to read Burlington crime is to separate the broad baseline from the categories moving inside it.

The baseline remains unusually low. The interesting story is where that starts to change.
