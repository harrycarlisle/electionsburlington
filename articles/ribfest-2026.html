Labour Day weekend

Ribfest turns 30,with nearly $6 million behind the smoke.

The waterfront festival is four days of smoke and music. It is also one of Burlington’s largest recurring fundraisers.

By Burlington NewsAugust 23, 20265-minute read

For four days every Labour Day weekend, the air around Burlington’s waterfront changes. Smoke drifts out of Spencer Smith Park, Lakeshore Road fills with people carrying paper trays, and a quiet patch of the city becomes a temporary barbecue town.

This year is the 30th anniversary of Canada’s Largest Ribfest. The festival runs from Friday, Sept. 4 through Monday, Sept. 7. The official schedule lists 11 a.m. to 11 p.m. Friday through Sunday and 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. on Labour Day. Gates close 30 minutes before the event ends.

Before you go
  • Sept. 4-7 at Spencer Smith Park
  • Friday-Sunday: 11 a.m.-11 p.m.
  • Monday: 11 a.m.-7 p.m.
  • Admission: $4 for ages 19-64, $2 for ages 13-18 and 65+, free for children 12 and under
  • Pets are not permitted, except guide dogs

The part the smoke can hide

Ribfest began in 1996 as a Rotary Club of Burlington Lakeshore project. The event’s own history describes it as Ontario’s first Ribfest. Thirty years later, it has become a major piece of Burlington’s public calendar, but the underlying purpose has not changed: festival proceeds are directed to charitable work.

Recent reporting from BurlingtonToday says the event has raised and donated close to $6 million. The list of organizations supported over the years includes the Burlington Food Bank, Food for Life, Carpenter Hospice, Community Living Burlington and the Joseph Brant Hospital Foundation and Auxiliary.

That makes the admission fee easier to understand. It is not a ticket to one performance or one plate of food. It is part of the fundraiser, while food and drinks are purchased separately from vendors.

What is different this year

The 2026 event is expected to bring 16 rib teams, a midway and a large covered tent with seating and live music. The entertainment lineup is spread across the weekend, with an ABBA tribute act scheduled as the Sunday-night headliner.

The most useful advice is also the least glamorous: check the official festival map before leaving, expect the waterfront to be busy, and do not assume the FAQ you remember from a previous year is still current. Event pages sometimes retain older schedule language even after the homepage has been updated.

Ribfest is easy to reduce to a photograph of ribs. The fuller Burlington story is what happens after the tents come down: money raised during four crowded days is redistributed through local groups for the rest of the year.
