Ribfest turns 30. Where does the money actually go?

Nearly $6 million has been raised over three decades, but the festival's impact goes beyond the food at Spencer Smith Park.

By Burlington NewsAugust 25, 20265-minute read
Topics:Events/Ribfest

For four days every Labour Day weekend, Spencer Smith Park becomes one of the easiest places in Burlington to identify without seeing it.

You smell it first.

Smoke drifts off the waterfront. Rib teams line the park. Music runs into the night. People carry paper trays toward the lake.

That is the part everyone remembers. The less visible part starts after the tents come down.

Before you go
  • Sept. 4-7 at Spencer Smith Park
  • Friday-Sunday: 11 a.m.-11 p.m.
  • Monday: 11 a.m.-7 p.m.
  • Admission: $4 for ages 19-64, $2 for ages 13-18 and 65+, free for children 12 and under
  • Food and drinks cost extra

The festival was built as a fundraiser

Canada's Largest Ribfest began in 1996 as a Rotary Club of Burlington Lakeshore fundraiser.

Thirty years later, it is one of Burlington's most recognizable annual events, but the basic model has not changed: create a weekend large enough to pull tens of thousands of people to the waterfront, then return part of what it generates to community causes.

Recent reporting puts the amount raised and donated over the festival's history at close to $6 million.

Organizations supported over the years include the Burlington Food Bank, Food for Life, Carpenter Hospice, Community Living Burlington and the Joseph Brant Hospital Foundation and Auxiliary.

So what is different for year 30?

The 2026 edition is scheduled to bring 16 rib teams, a midway, live music and a large covered seating area back to Spencer Smith Park from Friday, Sept. 4 through Labour Day Monday, Sept. 7.

The anniversary makes the event easy to frame as nostalgia: 30 years of smoke on the waterfront.

The better local story is that a temporary four-day festival became a recurring fundraising system whose effects continue after the park is empty.

The practical stuff still matters

Waterfront parking will be tight. Pets are not permitted except guide dogs. Gates close before the final listed event time. Food and drinks are separate from admission.

If you are going for a specific band or rib team, check the current official schedule before leaving rather than relying on an old social post.

If you are going because it is Ribfest, expect downtown and the waterfront to behave very differently from an ordinary September weekend.

The simplest way to understand Ribfest: the ribs are the attraction. The fundraiser is why the event exists.
