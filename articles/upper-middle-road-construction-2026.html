Upper Middle Road construction: what Burlington drivers should expect next.

The stretch between Mountain Grove Avenue and Guelph Line is moving into watermain work this fall. The road is not disappearing, but the easiest drive through it is about to change.

By Burlington NewsAug. 25, 20264-minute read

The important part of Burlington's Upper Middle Road project is not one construction date. It is that the work comes in stages, and drivers will feel each stage differently.

Phase One covers Upper Middle Road between Mountain Grove Avenue and Guelph Line. The City's current schedule moves the project into watermain installation in fall 2026, with that work expected to continue into spring 2027.

If this is part of your commute: expect lane restrictions and shifting work zones rather than one long full-road closure. Local access is supposed to remain available while crews move through the corridor.

The first disruption is underground

The watermain work is the first major construction phase residents will notice. Crews need room inside the roadway, so traffic lanes can be narrowed or shifted as the work advances.

That matters most at the times Upper Middle is already busy: school drop-off, the morning commute and the late-afternoon run toward Guelph Line. A route that looks open on a map can still take longer if a lane disappears around an active work zone.

Then the road itself gets rebuilt

Once the watermain phase is substantially complete, the project moves into surface restoration. The City currently schedules that work for spring through summer 2027.

The restoration phase includes resurfacing, sidewalk work, median construction and tree planting. In other words, finishing the watermain does not mean the corridor immediately returns to normal.

What residents should actually watch

The project timeline is useful, but daily traffic impacts will depend on where crews are working. The most useful updates are therefore the City's construction notices and temporary traffic-control changes, not simply the project's overall end date.

People who live directly on the corridor are expected to retain access to their homes. Through-drivers have more flexibility: on a bad day, the easiest way to avoid the work may be to shift north or south before reaching the active section rather than waiting until the lane restriction is in front of you.

Current schedule: watermain installation is planned from fall 2026 into spring 2027, followed by surface-restoration work through summer 2027. Construction schedules can change with weather, utilities and site conditions.
