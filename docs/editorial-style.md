# Burlington News editorial style

This is the human-readable companion to `data/editorial-policy.json`. Automation reads the JSON. Writers and later article passes should follow this page.

## One story, one goal

Every story should reduce to one sentence:

> This story helps a Burlington reader understand **X** because **Y changed**.

If that sentence is muddy, the story is too broad.

## Title and deck

- The title should be clear and curiosity-driven.
- The deck should create curiosity. It should not give away the full answer.
- Avoid generic local-news filler and weak listicles unless the subject is inherently a list.

## Story shape

1. Action
2. Consequence
3. The next tension or question

Once an answer is delivered, move to the next meaningful implication. Do not linger.

## Reader questions

Answer the obvious ones:

- where
- why
- how
- what changed
- why it matters
- what happens next

If a source does **not** disclose something, say that clearly instead of writing around the hole.

## Selection

Editorial selection should consider interest, familiarity, novelty, relevance, consequence and originality. Chronology alone should not choose the homepage. A single publisher should not become the implicit editor.

## Source transparency

- Social and community posts are discovery, never automatic proof.
- Corroborate important claims.
- Do not reuse publisher photography without permission or a documented licence.
- Maintain source links and rights credits.

## Auto-publish

Low-risk, verified items can publish when evidence standards are met. Hold:

- allegations
- serious crime claims
- candidate or political claims that require interpretation
- unclear community reports
- weakly corroborated stories

Do not auto-publish weak or unclear stories just for volume.
