# Burlington News editorial system

## Purpose
Burlington News should publish useful local stories that feel worth opening, not simply mirror another local outlet's homepage. Social posts, older reporting, public records, league results, City documents and community chatter can all start reporting. Evidence must support what gets published.

## Single-goal rule
Every story should be reducible to one sentence:

> This story helps a Burlington reader understand **X** because **Y changed**.

If that sentence is muddy, the story is too broad.

## Story shape
1. **Title:** clear, specific, curiosity-driven, with a promised payoff.
2. **Deck:** one or two sentences that explain the change and consequence.
3. **Curiosity intro:** begin with what changed or the surprising tension, not throat-clearing.
4. **Action -> consequence:** every section advances from what happened to what it changes.
5. **Next implication:** once one question is answered, move to the next consequence/conflict.
6. **Ending:** answer what happens next or what the reader should watch.

Avoid listicle framing unless the subject is inherently a list. Do not write generic 'everything you need to know' pieces.

## Homepage/editorial scoring
Stories compete on a 0-5 scale for:
- **Interest:** would a normal Burlington reader stop for this?
- **Relevance:** how directly does it affect Burlington?
- **Novelty:** is there something surprising/new?
- **Familiarity:** does the reader recognize the place/person/problem?
- **Consequence:** does it materially change something?
- **Breadth:** how many residents might care?
- **Source confidence:** primary/official > established reporting > corroborated community lead > single social post.
- **Originality:** does Burlington News add a useful angle, explanation or reporting rather than merely echoing another publisher?
- **Freshness:** new stories receive a temporary boost.
- **Rotation:** yesterday's lead should naturally fall unless it remains clearly strongest.

Straight aggregation is penalized. BurlingtonToday, InsideHalton, CHCH, CBC Hamilton, the Spectator and other outlets are useful discovery/reporting sources, but Burlington News should not become a rewrite layer for any one publisher.

## Discovery sources
Use a broad mix:
- City of Burlington / Halton Region / school boards / Metrolinx / Ontario 511 / police and public agencies
- Reddit and public Facebook posts as leads
- established local/regional reporting
- league standings, schedules, historical archives and older reporting
- local organizations, sports clubs, businesses and event organizers

A social post can begin reporting but does not become proof by itself.

## Example: local sports human story
A team approaching 0-24 can be a strong Burlington story even if it is not a conventional breaking-news event. It scores because it has:
- a simple human goal (can they avoid a winless season?)
- familiarity (local league/team/community)
- novelty (0-24 is unusually extreme)
- an easy visual/scoreboard concept
- a natural narrative arc

Older coverage of the team/players/league may be used as background, but current standings/results should be verified before publication.

## Automatic publishing policy
Low-risk stories may publish automatically when:
- evidence gate passes
- Burlington relevance gate passes
- image/usage rights gate passes
- duplicate gate passes
- verification is primary, official, or corroborated reporting
- story is not an allegation, accusation, endorsement, election persuasion, crime speculation, medical diagnosis or other high-risk claim

Sensitive stories should be held automatically rather than published from weak evidence.

## Writing prompt contract for future AI passes
When generating a story, provide:
- Topic
- Reader promise
- Main question
- Why it matters now
- Single story goal
- Source bundle
- Image/source-rights status

Output:
- 5 title options
- selected title
- deck
- story goal
- article body
- what happens next
- meta description
- share copy
- image concept

Writing rules:
- one main goal
- action -> consequence -> next implication
- no filler intros
- no generic local-news phrasing
- no unsupported claims
- no fake quotes
- do not overuse headings
- do not repeat the title/deck in the first paragraph
