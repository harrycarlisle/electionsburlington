# Headline and deck rubric

Score each candidate headline from 0–2 on every line. A publishable headline normally scores at least 10 of 14 and cannot score 0 for accuracy.

| Test | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Overstates or is unsupported | Technically true but imprecise | Exact and fully sourced |
| Clarity | Reader cannot predict the story | Understandable after rereading | Immediate promise |
| Specificity | Generic nouns and verbs | One concrete element | Concrete subject, action or number |
| Curiosity | Withholds the whole subject | Mild unanswered question | Strong factual open loop |
| Burlington relevance | City could be swapped out | Regional with a local angle | Burlington is essential |
| Human stakes | Abstract system only | Stakes implied | Reader can see who or what changes |
| Brevity and rhythm | Bloated or awkward | Serviceable | Clean aloud and strong on mobile |

## Required pass

1. Write at least five materially different headlines—not five adjective swaps.
2. Identify the most surprising verified fact in the reporting.
3. Test the headline at 375 CSS pixels. Avoid a final line containing one weak word.
4. Read it aloud. Remove bureaucratic phrases and unnecessary setup.
5. Ask whether the deck adds new information.
6. Search the archive for near-duplicate wording.
7. Run a final claim-to-source check after edits.

## Automatic rejections

- “Everything you need to know” when the story is not comprehensive.
- “What Burlington is saving,” “what this means,” or “the deeper challenge” without a concrete noun.
- A question the article does not answer.
- Sensational crime language unsupported by a court or public record.
- A number without enough context to be meaningful.
- “You won’t believe,” “shocking,” “game changer,” “hidden gem,” or “best kept secret.”

## Useful patterns

Use patterns only when the reporting naturally fits them:

- **The rejected alternative:** Ontario nearly replaced the Skyway with three tunnels.
- **The precise reversal:** Burlington built a bridge to remove trucks—then charged them to avoid it.
- **The local origin:** How one narrow menu became a Burlington ritual.
- **The documented time jump:** One Burlington day, 180 years apart.
- **The revealing count:** What 26,503 fish revealed about a damaged marsh.
