# What earns a Burlington News slot

This system decides placement. It is not a public popularity score and it never replaces editorial judgment.

Homepage slots now use separate clocks. See [homepage-ranking.md](../docs/homepage-ranking.md): Breaking Now is live, Newest is the last six hours, the hero is editorial, and Top Picks stays editorial until real readership data exists.

## First: hard gates

A story is ineligible if any of these fail:

- the factual core is not supported by reliable evidence;
- Burlington relevance is vague or incidental;
- the image rights or reconstruction label are unresolved;
- the item duplicates a story already occupying the same surface;
- a time-sensitive or seasonal window has ended.

Social engagement can identify a lead. It cannot pass an evidence gate.

## Then: five placement signals

Each eligible story is rated from 0–5 on five questions:

1. **Relevant now (30%)** — Does the reader need or want this today?
2. **Broadly useful (20%)** — How much of Burlington can use or care about it?
3. **Novel (20%)** — Does the reporting reveal something readers are unlikely to know?
4. **Familiar (15%)** — Is the person, place or experience recognizable enough to create an immediate connection?
5. **Consequential (15%)** — Does it change a decision, explain a stake or reward the reader's time?

The weighted result sorts eligible stories. It does not appear beside a headline. Editors still review the winning order for variety, duplicate subjects and visual quality.

## Different clocks for different stories

- **Current news** decays quickly and must have a verified publication or event date.
- **Service journalism** stays prominent only while readers can act on it.
- **Seasonal stories** have explicit start and end dates. When the window closes, they return to search and the archive.
- **Evergreen features** do not expire, but a homepage cooldown prevents the same feature from dominating indefinitely.

The weekly feature uses the same gates but values familiarity and novelty more heavily than day-to-day urgency. A strong feature can lead the page while the adjacent cards remain strictly current.

## Regional story test

“Burlington Stories” can include Hamilton, Oakville, Toronto or Canada only when the Burlington audience has a clear connection: a recognizable route or place, a local person, a direct consequence, or a regional event residents actually encounter. A generic GTA story does not become local because Burlington is added to the deck.

## Story lanes

- **Burlington Stories** is the default: reported stories rooted in a local person, place, route, institution or decision.
- **Around the Bay** covers Hamilton and Oakville only when Burlington readers share the consequence or recognize the experience.
- **Toronto Stories** is occasional narrative reporting with a concrete west-GTA connection. A public social post can be a tip, but the original poster must consent to being interviewed or the events must be independently documented.
- **Canada Stories** is rare. A national story earns space only when Burlington is more than a geographic afterthought.

Never lightly rewrite a Reddit, Facebook or X post and present it as journalism. Treat it as a lead, obtain permission where a private person's experience is central, verify the important facts and be explicit about what remains one person's account.

YouTube and other public videos are the same class of lead. See [video-leads.md](video-leads.md). They can inspire an independently reported explainer on a quiet news day. They cannot be paraphrased into a story and they cannot auto-publish.

## Coverage and bias review

Ground News compares how many left-, centre- and right-rated outlets cover the same event. Its ratings apply to publishers and are averaged from outside rating organizations; the coverage bar does not declare an individual story left- or right-wing.

Burlington News does not have enough independent local outlets covering every story to make a comparable public percentage honest. Instead, political and contested stories receive an internal coverage audit:

- identify primary records, interested parties and independent reporting;
- record who was asked to respond and who did not;
- check whether the headline adopts one side's framing;
- distinguish missing evidence from a missing opinion;
- avoid false balance when the underlying facts are not equally supported.

The result is better sourcing, not another badge competing for attention.
