Sources & methodology

How information gets onto the site, what gets checked, and what automation is allowed to do.

Source order

When sources disagree, the most direct record gets the most weight.

  • Official records: City of Burlington, Halton Region, election records, agendas, minutes, votes and formal decisions.
  • Candidate sources: campaign websites, direct statements and questionnaires for what a candidate says they support.
  • Local reporting: established reporting for interviews, events and additional context.
  • Community discussion: useful for finding questions and recurring concerns, but never treated as polling or proof by itself.

Burlington in 30 seconds

An automated monitor checks Burlington civic sources several times a day for changes involving City Hall, budgets, taxes, development, transit, housing, council decisions, elections and other local issues.

5Critical: voting deadlines, election results or major formal decisions with immediate citywide impact.4Major: budgets, major tax changes, major development or transit decisions.3Important: debates, significant platform changes, council votes and major public meetings.2Useful: relevant political or civic updates with narrower impact.1Reference: lower-priority background information.

The score controls prominence only. It is not a score of whether a politician, policy or story is good or bad.

Candidate information

Candidate names and ballot placement come from the City of Burlington's official list. A campaign statement is summarized as a proposal, not as something already achieved.

Candidate photos are shown only when the identity match is high-confidence. Candidate-supplied sites are checked first, then exact-name local candidate profiles. If the match is uncertain, the site keeps initials rather than guessing.

Local comments checked

We can quote substantive positive or critical comments from public discussions because they often surface questions residents are actually asking. The comment itself is not evidence.

We separate the opinion from any checkable factual claim and label the result: for example, supported, partly supported, core fact verified; motive unverified, or needs verification.

3Strong direct evidence supports the factual core.2Some direct evidence exists, but part of the claim is opinion, attribution or incomplete.1A checkable claim exists but the required record review is not complete.0No reliable evidence located; it should not be presented as fact.

The evidence score measures support for the factual part of a comment. It is not a popularity, truthfulness or candidate score.

Promises and track records

For people who have held office, a past commitment can be tracked when the original wording is specific enough to test and the dated source can be preserved. The outcome is then checked against public records.

We distinguish proposed, approved, funded, started and completed. Council-wide outcomes are not credited to one councillor unless the record shows a clear individual action. No overall promise-kept percentage should be published until there are enough reviewed commitments to make the number meaningful.

Ward lookup

This site does not guess a ward from a postal code. Because Burlington changed parts of Wards 1–4 for 2026, the ward finder uses the City of Burlington's official exact-address tool.

Images

Images must match the specific person or story. We do not insert generic or repeated images just to fill space. When reuse rights are unclear, omitting the image is safer than assuming permission.

Corrections

If something is wrong, outdated or misleadingly summarized, use Give feedback and include the page plus a stronger source. Corrections can be made while preserving the underlying source history.
