Toss Bosses went 0–24. Why do they keep coming back?

Toss Bosses have lost 24 straight league games, but the record does not explain why they still show up.

By Burlington News August 26, 2026 3 min read

Toss Bosses have now finished two straight regular seasons without a win.

Twelve losses last season. Twelve more this season. In Burlington Ultimate Club league play, that is 0–24.

How does a recreational team get there? And why do the same people still come out to throw a disc?

Burlington Ultimate Club posts schedules and standings for its rec divisions. That is how this streak became visible: not through a press conference, but through a table that kept adding losses.

How bad were the losses?

In this league, a game is played to 15. Across those 24 games, Toss Bosses never scored more than 7.

That is the only scoring detail Burlington News can stand behind. A public match-by-match sheet — closest game, worst blowout, whether the margins shrank in 2026 — was not available when this story was reported.

What those numbers do say is that Toss Bosses were not hanging around at 14–13. They were being beaten well before the other team reached 15.

Why are they losing?

Burlington Ultimate Club is a local rec league, not a professional circuit. Teams are made of people who work, miss weeks and still want a run-around on a grass field.

Public standings do not say whether Toss Bosses were short-handed, new to the sport, or simply placed against stronger sides. They also do not show roster changes, injuries or late-game execution.

The record can only take you so far. Talent, attendance, chemistry and a tough draw are all possible explanations. None of them can be proved from a win-loss line.

Why keep playing?

Ultimate is built around a culture that treats the scoreboard as only one of the reasons to be there. Games are self-officiated. Players call their own fouls. Rec leagues talk about spirit as if it were a stat, because for a weeknight team it often is.

A 0–24 side is still getting exercise, a weekly routine and a reason to see the same people. The streak can become part of the joke — not as humiliation, but as a shared project: get one.

Burlington News did not find public player comments that would let us put that in anyone’s mouth. The pattern is still familiar in this city. People stay for the group long after the standings have given up on them.

The club also scheduled Toss Bosses for a playoff game outside those regular-season standings. A win there would be a real result. It would not rewrite 0–24.

The regular-season line is closed. Watch whether this group comes back, whether the club puts them in a different competitive slot, and whether Toss Bosses finally get on the board next season.
