How bad is crime in Burlington, really?

Halton is unusually safe. Fraud is the category that will not sit still.

By Burlington News Staff August 25, 2026 Updated August 26, 2026

Direct answer. Burlington is not getting more dangerous on the best comparable numbers. Halton’s 2024 Crime Severity Index was 31.2, well below Hamilton, Toronto and nearby large services. In 2025 Halton recorded fewer total crimes. Property crime, auto theft and break-and-enter fell. Fraud and theft over $5,000 rose. Those are regional police-service totals, not a Burlington-only score, and they do not rank neighbourhoods.

What happened

If you ask whether Burlington is getting dangerous, the easiest statistic has an annoying problem: Burlington does not get its own Crime Severity Index.

Burlington is policed by Halton Regional Police along with Oakville, Milton and Halton Hills. Statistics Canada therefore publishes the cleanest police-service comparison at the Halton level, not as a Burlington-only score.

That means we cannot honestly put a number beside “Burlington” and pretend it describes only this city. We can still use Halton to answer the broader question.

The Crime Severity Index, or CSI, is not a raw crime count. It gives more weight to more serious offences, which makes it useful for comparing places with different populations and mixes of crime.

Halton’s CSI was 31.2 in 2024, compared with 66.2 in Hamilton and 68.7 in Toronto. Nearby large services were all higher.

So the broad picture is not ambiguous: Burlington sits inside an unusually low-crime large policing region. That is also why a frightening individual incident does not automatically prove a crime wave.

2025 moved in two directions

Halton police recorded 15,673 total crimes in 2025, down 1.8% from the year before. Property crime fell 4.3%. Auto theft dropped 20.1%. Break-and-enter fell 10.8%.

If the story stopped there, “crime fell” would sound reasonable.

The categories that rose are the more interesting ones. Reported fraud climbed 12.7%, to 2,537 incidents. Theft over $5,000 jumped 68.1%, to 459. Violent crime edged higher, from 2,377 incidents to 2,420.

Fraud is the number that keeps climbing

This is not a one-year blip. Halton’s reported fraud count has risen every year since 2021: from 1,017 to 1,411, 1,554, 2,252 and then 2,537. The 2024 jump was already large.

Halton police have not published a verified reason for that latest 12.7% increase. The public crime tables show the count. They do not explain the cause.

What police and national fraud agencies do describe is a different kind of property crime. Halton’s frauds-and-scams guidance is built around telephone, romance, cryptocurrency and remote-access bank-investigator schemes. The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre says Canadians reported more than 112,000 frauds in 2025, with losses above $704 million. Its highest-impact categories include fake investment platforms, romance schemes, identity theft and phishing emails or texts. “There are no broken windows or visible crime scenes,” the Centre writes.

That does not prove those methods produced Halton’s extra 285 fraud reports. It does show what police and national agencies say the current fraud problem looks like.

Why it matters in Burlington

A Halton CSI of 31.2 cannot tell you whether one Burlington neighbourhood has more break-ins than one in Oakville. It can tell you that Burlington’s overall policing environment is not Toronto’s or Hamilton’s.

The 2025 numbers add a second, more local point. Traditional property crime — cars, houses, mischief — moved down. Fraud kept climbing, and high-value theft climbed with it.

Halton’s traditional property-crime numbers moved down in 2025. Fraud did not. Reported fraud rose again, to 2,537 incidents — the category worth watching next. Police warnings and national fraud data both describe scams that reach people by phone, text, email and online platforms rather than a broken window or a stolen car. Whether that is why Halton’s count keeps rising is still the unanswered part.

What we don’t know

These tables cannot say whether one Burlington street is safer than another. They cannot separate Oakville, Milton, Halton Hills and Burlington inside the CSI. They cannot prove why fraud rose 12.7% in 2025. They also cannot tell you how much crime went unreported.

What happens next

The next maintainable checkpoint is Halton police’s 2026 annual crime tables and Statistics Canada’s next CSI release for Ontario police services. This page will be updated when those numbers are published. Until then, treat 2025 as the current annual record, not a neighbourhood ranking. Related hub: Public safety.
