How bad is Burlington's crime, really?

Burlington has a reputation for being safe. The best comparable police data shows why that reputation exists, but one detail makes the answer less simple than a single city ranking.

By Burlington NewsAugust 25, 20266-minute read

If you ask whether Burlington is getting dangerous, the first problem is surprisingly basic: the most comparable national crime number is not published for Burlington by itself.

Burlington is policed by the Halton Regional Police Service, along with Oakville, Milton and Halton Hills. Statistics Canada therefore publishes the cleanest police-service comparison at the Halton level, not as a Burlington-only score.

That distinction matters. But it does not make the comparison useless.

The clearest signal: Halton's overall Crime Severity Index was 31.2 in 2024. Among the large Ontario police services compared by Halton police, that was the lowest figure in the group.

The gap is not small

The Crime Severity Index, or CSI, is different from simply counting offences. It weighs crimes according to their seriousness, so a more severe offence matters more to the index than a minor one.

In 2024, Halton's CSI was 31.2. Durham was 47.1. York was 53.9. Peel was 56.4. Hamilton was 66.2, Toronto 68.7 and Waterloo 72.7.

That means the best apples-to-apples regional measure does support the idea that Burlington sits inside an unusually low-crime policing area.

It also helps explain why comparing raw incident headlines can be misleading. A city can have a frightening individual crime, or a cluster of thefts, without suddenly resembling a higher-crime region overall.

But 2025 did not move every kind of crime in the same direction

Halton police recorded 15,673 total crimes in 2025, down 1.8% from 15,967 in 2024.

Property crime moved down more clearly. The regional total fell 4.3%, from 12,455 to 11,925. Auto theft fell 20.1%, break-and-enter fell 10.8%, and theft under $5,000 fell 5.5%.

Then there is the other side of the story.

Violent crime rose 1.8%, from 2,377 reported incidents to 2,420. Assaults increased 2.7%. Robberies were unchanged at 132. Halton also recorded three homicides in 2025 after recording none in 2024.

So the useful conclusion is not that "crime is down" or "crime is up." Different categories moved in different directions.

Why Burlington cannot honestly be ranked against Toronto by one city number

Toronto and Hamilton have police services whose published figures line up much more closely with their namesake cities. Halton's police service covers four municipalities.

That means it would be misleading to label Halton's 31.2 CSI as "Burlington's CSI" and put it beside Toronto as though the geography were identical.

The regional comparison still tells us something important about the environment Burlington is in. It just cannot tell us whether one Burlington neighbourhood has more theft, assault or break-ins than one neighbourhood in Oakville.

For that, incident maps and more local police data are better tools than the regional CSI.

What should a Burlington resident actually take from this?

The strongest comparable data does not support the idea that Burlington is experiencing crime on the same scale as Toronto or Hamilton. Halton has remained at the low end of Ontario's large police-service CSI comparisons.

At the same time, "safe" does not mean nothing is changing. Fraud reached 2,537 reported incidents across Halton in 2025, up 12.7%. Violent crime edged higher even while total crime and property crime fell.

That is probably the more useful way to watch Burlington crime: not by treating every incident as evidence of a wave, and not by assuming a low regional score means every category is improving.

The broad picture remains unusually low. The interesting story is what changes inside it.
