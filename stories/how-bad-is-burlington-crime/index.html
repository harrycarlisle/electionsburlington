How bad is Burlington's crime, really?

Burlington has a reputation for being safe. The clearest comparable data suggests that reputation is broadly deserved — but the details matter.

By Burlington News August 25, 2026 4 min read

If you ask whether Burlington is getting dangerous, the easiest statistic has an annoying problem: Burlington does not get its own Crime Severity Index.

Burlington is policed by Halton Regional Police along with Oakville, Milton and Halton Hills. Statistics Canada therefore publishes the cleanest police-service comparison at the Halton level, not as a Burlington-only score.

That means we cannot honestly put a number beside “Burlington” and pretend it describes only this city. We can still use Halton to answer the broader question.

The clearest signal: Halton’s overall Crime Severity Index was 31.2 in 2024 — the lowest among the large Ontario police services Halton police compared.

The gap is large

The Crime Severity Index, or CSI, is not a raw crime count. It gives more weight to more serious offences, which makes it useful for comparing places with different populations and mixes of crime.

Halton’s 31.2 sits well below nearby large services. Toronto was more than twice as high. Waterloo was higher still.

So the broad picture is not ambiguous: Burlington sits inside an unusually low-crime large policing region. That is also why a frightening individual incident does not automatically prove a crime wave.

2025 did not move in one direction

Halton police recorded 15,673 total crimes in 2025, a modest 1.8% decline from the year before. That total hides a split.

Improved Property crime −4.3% Auto theft fell 20.1%
Moved higher Violent crime +1.8% Fraud rose 12.7%

Total crimes: 15,673, down from 15,967 in 2024.

If the story stopped at property crime, “crime fell” would sound reasonable. Violent crime edged higher — 2,377 reported incidents to 2,420 — and fraud rose more sharply. A total can hide all three movements at once.

The useful answer is not simply “up” or “down.” Property crime and auto theft improved. Violent crime edged higher. Fraud rose.

Why Toronto is not a perfect comparison

Toronto and Hamilton have police services whose published statistics line up much more closely with their namesake cities. Halton covers four municipalities.

That makes it misleading to label Halton’s 31.2 CSI as “Burlington’s CSI.” The regional comparison still tells us something important about Burlington’s overall policing environment. It cannot say whether one Burlington neighbourhood has more break-ins than one in Oakville.

So how worried should a Burlington resident be?

The strongest comparable data does not support the idea that Burlington is experiencing crime on the same scale as Toronto or Hamilton.

It also does not support complacency. A low regional score does not mean every category is improving, and it does not make an individual victim’s experience less real.

The baseline remains unusually low. The interesting story is where that starts to change.
